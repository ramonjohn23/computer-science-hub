03 | WHY USE PACKETS?

Five reasons to divide data

1. Easier to transmit

Smaller units can be handled across the network.

2. Different routes

Packets can travel through different available routes.

3. Resending

If one packet is lost, only that packet needs to be resent.

4. Shared resources

Network resources can be shared.

5. Router management

Routers can manage and forward packets.

06 | PACKET SWITCHING

What is packet switching?

Packet switching is a method of data transmission in which data is divided into packets, and the packets can travel through different routes to reach the destination.

P1 → R1 → R3 → Computer B
P2 → R2 → R4 → R3 → Computer B
P3 → R1 → R3 → Computer B

Different packets from the same message may take different paths through the network.

06 | ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES

Packet switching

Flexible and efficient, but can be unpredictable.

If one road is busy, a packet can take another road. Packets may arrive at different times or in the wrong order.

Advantages

  • Efficient use of the network.
  • Can use different routes.
  • Reliable.
  • Suitable for large amounts of data.

Disadvantages

  • Packets may arrive out of order.
  • Packets may be delayed.
  • Packets need additional information.
  • Data must be reassembled.

07 | VIDEO CONFERENCE PROBLEMS

What problems might users experience?

📺 Blurry video

The picture may look unclear or break into blocks.

🔊 Sound breaks up

The sound may cut in and out or not match the video.

⏱️ Delays (lag)

There may be a noticeable delay between speaking and seeing or hearing the other person.

⏸️ Video freezes

The video may stop temporarily and then continue.

07 | CAUSES

Why do these problems happen?

Network congestion

Too much data on the network causes packets to be delayed.

Packets take different routes

Packets may arrive out of order or at different times.

Packet loss

Some packets may be lost if the network is busy or unstable, so parts of video or sound are missing.

Limited bandwidth

The connection speed may not be high enough to handle high-quality video and sound.

Long distance

Data travelling a very long distance can increase delay.
